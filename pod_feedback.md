# Pod Feedback — Week 5 Diagnostics Presentation

**Presenter:** Alex Kariuki  
**Pod:** Oil & Gas  
**Session date:** July 31, 2026  
**Format:** Live pod review — each member presented findings for 5 minutes, remaining members acted as Sceptical Directors.

---

## Feedback Received

### Question 1 — "How do you know the Beta pressure drop wasn't just sensor error?"

**Asked by:** Pod member (Sceptical Director role)

**Original response:** I pointed to the fact that production dropped in lockstep with pressure — if it were a sensor fault, the physical output numbers wouldn't have moved.

**Feedback:** The pod noted my answer was correct but delivered too quickly. They suggested I slow down and lead with the production data first (the number the Director cares about), then explain the pressure correlation as supporting evidence rather than the main argument.

**How I improved:** I restructured the argument in my final response to open with *"Beta lost 40% of its daily output over 91 consecutive days — that is not sensor noise"* before introducing the pressure statistics. This reframing made the answer land more clearly with a non-technical audience.

---

### Question 2 — "Could the Delta problems just be summer heat affecting the equipment?"

**Asked by:** Pod member (Sceptical Director role)

**Original response:** I cited the temperature correlation figure (r = 0.08) as evidence that weather was not the driver.

**Feedback:** The pod pointed out that citing a correlation coefficient to a Director without context is jargon. I needed to translate it into plain language.

**How I improved:** I rewrote the answer to say: *"We tested that specifically. If summer heat were the cause, we'd expect downtime to rise and fall with temperature across all fields. It doesn't — temperature and downtime move independently in our data, while equipment failure count tracks downtime almost perfectly. The problem is mechanical, not environmental."* The pod agreed this version was significantly clearer and more persuasive.

---

### Question 3 — "These two problems look like bad luck. Why should we invest in a monitoring system?"

**Asked by:** Pod member (Sceptical Director role)

**Original response:** I explained that earlier detection would have shortened the Beta crisis window.

**Feedback:** The pod felt this was weak because it didn't quantify the value. They pushed me to attach a number to the argument.

**How I improved:** I added the following to my argument: *"Beta's pressure crisis ran for 91 days. With an automated pressure alert set at 70% of baseline, the trigger would have fired within the first week. If we cut the crisis window from 91 days to 10 days, we recover approximately 80% of the production loss — which is several million barrels. The cost of a monitoring alert is trivial against that recovery."* The pod rated this version as the strongest answer of the session.

---

## Overall Reflection

The main theme across all three challenges was the same: I defaulted to technical precision when the audience needed business clarity. The Sceptical Director exercise forced me to translate every finding into consequence and cost before the argument landed. Going forward, I will structure every findings presentation by leading with the business impact first and holding the statistical evidence in reserve as supporting detail rather than the headline.
